---
name: Summarizing conflicting evidence
trigger: conflicting, contradicts, disagree, some sentences say, opposite effect, inconsistent
---
When the fetched supporting sentences disagree with each other (e.g. some describe a positive/
activating effect and others a negative/inhibitory one, or describe the interaction under different
conditions), do not silently pick a side or average them into a vague middle statement. Instead:

- Say plainly that the evidence is mixed.
- Attribute each side to what the sentence(s) actually say, briefly (e.g. "activating in the
  presence of X, inhibitory otherwise" or "described as protective in two sentences and harmful in
  one") rather than just counting how many sentences said what.
- If the disagreement looks like it's explained by a difference in context mentioned in the
  sentences themselves (cell type, dose, disease state, experimental model), say so — that is
  itself useful information, not noise to average away.
- Never resolve the conflict using outside/pretrained knowledge — only what the fetched sentences
  themselves say about why they might differ.
